About

bodymodarchive is a literary & artistic archive for true (or mostly-true) stories around medical procedures and medical experiences, especially those that are less talked about or otherwise underrepresented. Anyone who has ever used birth control, received gender-affirming care, gone to therapy, received abortion care, been diagnosed with an autoimmune disease, undergone chemotherapy, been misdiagnosed, tried IVF, and so on — likely has a story that they’ve either shared only with their close friends or kept to themselves, whether that’s due to stigma, emotional weight, or just not knowing whether now is the right time to share.

And yet, every time I share one of my harrowing medical experiences as a regrettable party anecdote, I’m inevitably greeted with a deluge of people excited to share similar stories—both good and bad—gruesome or hopeful. Often, the stories don’t share the same takeaway, psychological impact, severity, or even level of tact with which they’re told, but they do create a moment where everyone seems to realize that their experiences are a little less isolated than they might have thought before. Like cavepeople sitting around a fire. When this conversation ends, I’m struck with a feeling of transience, as the stories, for the most part, leave the party with the people who brought them, soon to dissipate from the collective memory, and making me wonder if what just happened really happened at all.

So each of us passes through rooms with dozens, if not hundreds, of these stories each day, and yet when people need to undergo a new procedure, they’ve often nowhere to turn except their tiktok algorithms (rabbit-holing), subreddits (terrifying), WebMD (worse), or published medical papers (a horrendously incomplete literature). While bodymodarchive doesn’t profess to solve the issues of underrepresentation in the actual field of medical research, it hopes to add to a collective memory. In other words, it hopes to recreate that ephemeral feeling of people sitting around a fire, telling different versions of the same story.

And possibly, make it a little less ephemeral.
